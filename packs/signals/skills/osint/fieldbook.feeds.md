# Expert feeds, reading rooms, and people worth studying for method

Some of the best sources are **specialist publications that explain *why* things matter**, not just data endpoints. They help an agent discover research questions, criticism, vocabulary, and unexplored source routes. A desk should learn from their craft rather than merely paraphrase their output. Subscriber content is licensed—**do not treat a login or URL as permission to scrape or redistribute**.

## Professional desks worth studying as *models of work product*

| Research desk / publication | Distinctive excellence | What PE can learn | Access / caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [SemiAnalysis](https://semianalysis.com/about/) and [Core Research](https://semianalysis.com/core-research/) | Bottom-up semiconductor and AI supply-chain analysis, physical/technical constraints, economic models, field visits, continuous revision. | The **product → component → capacity → cost → firm exposure** chain; a weekly brief with the right deep models linked underneath. | Free and subscriber articles, separate institutional model licenses. Industry assertions still require checking. |
| [SemiAnalysis Panther Lake teardown](https://newsletter.semianalysis.com/p/intel-panther-lake-teardown) | A concrete 2026 example of evaluating a technology claim by inspecting shipped hardware, not repeating corporate roadmaps. | What original technical due diligence looks like: primary artifact, method, measurements, implications and limitations. | Technical findings should be interpreted in context; commercial reproduction isn't automatically licensed. |
| [The Information Pro](https://www.theinformation.com/pro) | Reporting and proprietary databases, described on its public product page; subscriber tools not evaluated. | A newsroom can build **reusable proprietary knowledge objects** out of years of reporting, not just one-day articles. | Paid; do not extract proprietary data without authorized rights. |
| [Techmeme](https://www.techmeme.com/about) | Dense editorial selection, linked multiple perspectives, and prioritization across a constantly moving stream. | How to make a short briefing that directs attention rather than floods it. | Its rankings optimize editorial relevance, not the operator's unique opportunity. |
| [Stratechery](https://stratechery.com/about/) | Durable strategic frameworks applied to technology news through recurring analytical updates. | Reuse an explicit strategic model across many events, and be willing to change the model when evidence fails. | Free weekly and subscriber updates. Analysis is interpretation, not source fact. |
| [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/about/) | Original reporting on platforms, privacy, consumer rights, security and technology's societal effects. | Investigative tech storytelling that starts with an original lead rather than a trend recap. | Editorial subscription/access may vary; source-based verification required. |
| [Industry Dive](https://www.industrydive.com/what-to-expect-from-our-newsletters) | Industry-specific daily/weekly briefs with quick takeaways, deep dives and weekend reviews. | An example of a standing service: fast awareness + slower specialty research + periodic synthesis. | Specific titles vary; advertising/sponsorship and a publication's selection priorities differ from PE's. |
| [PitchBook research process](https://pitchbook.com/help/pitchbooks-research-process) | Continuous entity discovery, verification, revision and corrective feedback. | A desk learns by **updating records**, not merely accumulating summaries. | Licensed product and primary source work not freely available. |
| [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/faqs) | Dataset lineage, variable definitions, accessible analysis and source methods. | High-quality numerical research needs interpretable provenance and comparability. | Check individual datasets' attribution/license and vintage. |

### A particularly useful research model: SemiAnalysis

Its [documented research process](https://semianalysis.com/core-research/) connects technical teardowns, supply-chain forensics, industry models and company implications. The valuable PE lesson isn't that every lab needs an expensive chip teardown. It is that **different kinds of evidence constrain the same thesis**: physical mechanisms limit credible supply, suppliers limit delivery, customers constrain demand, and market claims need to reconcile with all of them. A second specialist desk can follow a different layer without losing context.

## Timely *official* reading rooms (more than press headlines)

| Route | What may arrive early | Opportunity / limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Federal Register public inspection](https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/public-inspection) | Regulatory material available before formal publication. | Early assessment of policy changes; still distinguish proposed, final and effective. |
| [SEC EDGAR APIs](https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces) | Earnings, 8-K filings, annual/quarterly records, insider/ownership filings. | Near-original company evidence; verify amendments and distinguish reporting from underlying event timing. |
| [Congress.gov API](https://api.congress.gov/) | Legislative stages and documents. | Surface near-term policy research questions; bills are not enacted law. |
| [GovInfo feeds](https://www.govinfo.gov/developers) | Official government publications and collections. | Calendar-driven policy research; each collection has its own lag. |
| [ClinicalTrials.gov API](https://clinicaltrials.gov/data-api/api) | Registry changes, results, dates and trial descriptions. | Trial design / endpoint evolution; registry update doesn't equal scientific evidence. |
| [FDA open data](https://open.fda.gov/apis/) | Device, drug and enforcement event data. | Emerging regulatory anomalies; adverse-event report bias is substantial. |
| [NBER weekly working papers](https://www.nber.org/papers) | New research questions and empirical methods. | Useful for slow evening/weekend work; preliminary papers may not be peer reviewed. |
| [IETF Datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/) | Draft protocols and working-group discussions. | Product/standards research before launch; drafts may die. |
| [GH Archive](https://www.gharchive.org/) | Historical public repository events. | New projects and engineering changes before media coverage; many events are automated. |
| [CISA KEV catalog](https://github.com/CISAgov/kev-data) | Evidence-based listing of known-exploited CVEs. | Security prioritization, not proof that unlisted CVEs are unexploited. |

## An example of a reading rotation, not a new scheduler

**Morning:** official overnight disclosures, verified critical alerts, change summaries from standing sources. **Daytime:** original records when something changes; triage and hypothesis checks, not reading every newsletter. **Evening:** one or two specialist deep dives (e.g. SemiAnalysis method or an ICIJ source trail). **Weekend:** source discovery, missed-lead review, new relationships and updates to field guides.

The operator defines which actual reports are expected; PE's manager owns task and fleet allocation. These reading ideas **do not create time triggers or host jobs**. Subscription feeds should be used by ordinary authorized readers or official feeds/APIs, not indiscriminate scraping.

## How to decide a newsletter deserves to stay

Track two separate reasons to read it: **unique early facts** and **superior interpretation**. The first may predict material events; the second might improve the lab's explanatory models without ever being early. A source that contributes nothing but repeated headlines gets downgraded. One exceptional counterexample can be more valuable than 100 routine posts.
