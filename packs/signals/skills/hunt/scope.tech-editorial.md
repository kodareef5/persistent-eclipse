# Scope: Technology editorial and SEO strategy

Find opportunities to help a technical audience solve real problems. Use community
interactions, search questions, customer analysis, and competitor research to
shape timely ideas and a durable content strategy. Draw on it when drafting
`.research/SCOPE.md`; keep what fits the operator's request and fill in what they
actually asked for.

## What counts

- Salience: whether the audience is starting to discuss or search for the topic.
- Significance: whether it represents a consequential or recurring audience need.
- Opportunity: something this publication can explain or investigate usefully,
  given its expertise and resources. Timeliness helps some ideas; durable value
  helps others. Being first is not required.

Community interactions are leads to needs and language, not a representative
sample of demand. Distinguish independent reports from copied complaints. Do not
invent keyword volumes or infer customer prevalence from a few posts.

## Research agenda

Follow community questions and replies, including unresolved discussions and
workarounds. Collect effective explanatory concepts with their source and the
reason they work. Investigate long-tail phrasing and search intent, compare
competitor coverage against the actual problem, and examine customer needs and
objections. Respect source wording and attribution; reuse an idea through original
analysis rather than copying a competitor's prose.

Connect individual opportunities into a longer-term topic plan: prerequisites,
audience journey, related questions, gaps in existing coverage, and what should be
updated, combined, or retired. A competitor's omission is an opening only if it
serves a real need and the publication can contribute something useful.

## Deliverables

A short radar, researched opportunity briefs, and a rolling topic plan. A brief
names the audience problem, evidence, long-tail questions, competitor/customer
insights, useful angle, strongest objection, and how it fits the plan. Develop an
outline when the research supports one; list the original checks or expertise
needed to make it credible. Limited samples can demonstrate a difficult explanation
or proposed format. Full articles and publication volume are not the default work.

## Forecasts and feedback

Optional families: HN front-page appearances as in the HN Scout scope, or whether
named competitors cover a specific topic by a date. Resolve the latter manually
from dated extracts. An idea can be useful without being forecastable.

Record which briefs the operator uses, whether they answer the intended question,
which assumptions fail, and subsequent audience feedback. Traffic, rankings, and
conversion depend on execution and distribution as well as research quality.

## Vocabulary and first project

Audience, task, intent, question, angle, category, pillar, coverage gap, competitor,
content asset. Collect relevant discussion and competitor links; group them by
reader question, then compare with the publication's existing content. Add page
change monitoring when repeated use warrants it. Protect customer and long-tail
research when timely stories are plentiful. See [audience investigations](plays.md).

## Report contract

Deliver a daily opportunity report at *(edit: local time and timezone)*, covering
new evidence since the prior cutoff. Save it to
`.research/reports/editorial/<YYYY-MM-DD>/<report-id>-opportunities.md`.
Use these headings; state cutoff and monitored beats below the title:

```markdown
# Editorial opportunity report
## Opportunities and who needs them
## How we found them and what existing coverage misses
## Research advanced and competing explanations
## Recommended angles, outlines, and original checks
## Updates, pillar connections, and next research
```

Include source links, sampling limits, and reasons to decline weak ideas. Agree
separate short formats for weekly planning and monthly pillar reviews; link to
underlying research and save them as `<report-id>-weekly.md` or `-pillars.md`.

## Sustained investigation artifacts

Audience maps, customer/intent studies, competitor comparisons, topic architectures,
and researched outlines can take their own form. Keep reviewed work under
`.research/reports/investigations/<topic>/`, with source/sample limits, competing
interpretations, original checks still needed, and agreed review checkpoints.
Daily briefs surface the useful changes and link to these artifacts. Outline only
supported opportunities; short samples can clarify an approach. A calendar assigns
potential work, not an article-production quota.

## Authorization

Research and recommendation. No publication, outreach, pitches, or community posts.
