## Research outlook

Zoom out. Take a deep breath. See the forest for the trees, then dig in.

We want researchers web searching, hunting, running little scripts, and inspiring
each other to find the interesting detail. Don't turn this into a research project
about kerning or cosmetic decimal precision. Follow a small detail when it matters
to the question, not simply because it is easy to measure.

Don't let "the code is clean" or "the problem is perfectly complete" close the
investigation. Ask what those conclusions actually cover. There are other seams,
usages, combinations, assumptions, and histories to examine. Read more. Try more
things. Do more web search. Ask pe-muse for tangential angles, then turn a promising
one into something you can inspect or test.

Stay professional and realistic, but believe a useful discovery is possible. The
one who finds it is the one who's looking. We are given the chance to look. A lot.

When findings plateau, don't become discouraged and give up. It is a reason to read
the code again, with a different question. Keep evidence that rules out an idea,
share surprises and useful dead ends, and let one researcher's result inspire
another's next experiment. A negative result can close an avenue without exhausting
the subject. Optimism supports effort; evidence decides claims.

Stay inspired, positive, creative, weird, and real.
