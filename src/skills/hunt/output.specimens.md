# Output specimens: what *good* and *weak* research looks like

All cases and values below are **fictional teaching examples**, not live market, legal, biomedical, company or web findings. The point is **shape, evidentiary discipline and usefulness**: good shapes to aim for, not schemas to copy. Standing service outputs may have predictable headings; deep investigation artifacts should follow the subject.

## 1. Financial pre-market desk: one-screen report, with doors into deeper work

### Weak

> **Market Morning:** Metals are rallying. Several companies discussed expansion. AI-related stocks may see gains. Monitor earnings and geopolitical events. Rare earths could be the next big opportunity.

Why weak: no named change, no primary source, no distinction between observations and inference, no impact on the operator's watched positions, no explanation of what action the analyst should take.

### Stronger specimen

> **Pre-bell desk · Hypothetical Monday, 07:20 ET**
>
> **What needs attention**
>
> **1. Capacity thesis reopened — medium urgency.** Company A's newly published facility update pushes the expected commissioning milestone by one quarter. This affects our earlier assumption that added supply would arrive before a customer's procurement renewal. Primary source: company facility notice [SOURCE-1]; previous forecast [F-12]. **Research implication:** revise the downstream shortage scenario, not the entire company thesis.
>
> **2. Watchlist unaffected so far.** Related policy headlines are circulating, but no official final rule or change in the affected HS categories was found in the sources checked by 07:10 ET. Source: public-inspection notices and current tariff schedule [SOURCE-2, SOURCE-3]. **Don't infer** there is no future measure.
>
> **3. New specialty candidate — low conviction.** An academic abstract describes a different processing technique. We don't yet know whether it is economically scalable. Assign a narrow feasibility review when a slot opens; don't promote to an investment idea yet.
>
> **Strongest counterargument:** reporting dates and commercial operation dates can diverge; public update may not capture trial commissioning already underway.
>
> **Next:** verify source timing, request independent challenge on the procurement assumption, keep ordinary portfolio watch active.
>
> **Unchanged from prior brief:** no new evidence affecting two other current high-priority theses. [Not repeated here; see desk status.]

**Why it's better:** operator-specific priority, verified/unknown separation, stable links to accumulated intelligence, a genuinely new hypothesis, proportional escalation, and limited length. The report is short because the **deep record lives elsewhere**.

## 2. Deep diligence artifact: conditional thesis, not a persuasive essay

### Weak

> Company A has strong management and many growth catalysts. New infrastructure and political demand imply a large upside. Our agents agree it is the best-positioned beneficiary.

### Stronger specimen: the hinge brief

| Component | Example finding |
| --- | --- |
| **Thesis** | Company A benefits materially if constrained processing supply persists through the buyer's next procurement cycle. |
| **Direct evidence** | Named public project milestones; official trade figures; disclosed customer mix. Each recorded with as-of date. |
| **Key assumptions** | Capacity and product quality meet buyer specification; downstream prices are not completely fixed; competitor B doesn't commission early. |
| **Competing interpretation** | Project delay is temporary and switching costs are lower than assumed. |
| **Conditional worlds** | **A:** bottleneck persists → potential price/margin sensitivity. **B:** capacity arrives → thesis weakens. **C:** substitution accelerates → alternative beneficiaries emerge. |
| **Critical unknown** | Public evidence does not establish how much usable qualified output reaches the buyer by renewal. |
| **Next highest-value investigation** | Identify the relevant product-grade qualification path and whether any official disclosure would reveal it. |
| **Position** | *Research thesis only*, not a trade instruction or valuation. |
| **Reopening trigger** | Facility certification, supplier filings, or material revision to capacity figures. |

**Why it's better:** multiple futures remain plausible; value of the next evidence is explicit; conviction is not confused with commercial upside. A future worker knows exactly what to investigate without starting over.

## 3. Editorial desk: pitches with differentiated angles

### Weak

> Publish an article about AI coding agents; it's trending. Good for SEO. Also publish five related articles and a comparison.

### Stronger specimen

| Pitch | Original reporting opportunity | Reader benefit | Objection / gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Agent memory that actually survives a failed run** | Build a controlled crash-and-resume comparison across three open-source harness designs; publish transcripts/configuration and observed outcomes. | A developer can select a research workflow on evidence rather than marketing. | Requires reproducible tests; without them, it's another generic AI opinion piece. |
| **What changed in browser automation this month** | Trace changed APIs/compatibility notes back to release diffs, with a platform support matrix. | Operators can decide whether to upgrade today. | If changes affect only obscure environments, combine into a brief rather than full feature. |
| **The neglected category behind five separate complaints** | Independently reproduce public symptoms from unrelated repos, establish whether they share a mechanism, and, only with operator authorization, ask maintainers about the unresolved mechanism. | Names a practical problem and offers a diagnosis nobody has assembled. | Might be coincidences; don't claim a pattern until code evidence supports it. |

**Why it's better:** every candidate has an *investigative act* that makes the story defensible and a clear reject condition. SEO demand is checked only after the unique reader benefit has been identified.

A scalable editorial team can attach estimated audience fit, urgency, resources required and source confidence. It shouldn't turn those subjective choices into a fake “92.7 opportunity score.”

## 4. Legal desk: adverse authority alert that changes a real argument

### Weak

> New court case found. It may affect your motion. Please review.

### Stronger specimen

> **Matter alert · hypothetical case**
>
> **Authority changed:** A newly published appellate opinion discusses the exception our argument relies on. Source: official published opinion at [COURT-SOURCE], verified against current docket at [DOCKET].
>
> **Existing proposition affected:** R-72, “The exception applies whenever condition X is established.”
>
> **New tension:** The opinion distinguishes condition X where additional fact Y is absent. Our available record does not yet establish whether Y exists.
>
> **Adverse interpretation:** Opposing counsel could argue R-72 overstates the holding for this jurisdiction and posture.
>
> **Requested attorney decision:** Confirm case applicability, binding status, procedural timing, and whether to narrow the planned argument.
>
> **What the lab did not determine:** No deadline recalculation; no new filing; no claim that the opinion automatically controls this matter.

**Why it's better:** legal research is presented as a change to a **specific relied-on proposition**, with an explicit review request. It can be challenged line by line and does not convert discovery into unauthorized legal action.

## 5. OSINT field note: one source method worth preserving

### Weak

> [Platform URL] — good website to check for market intelligence. Updated Oct 31.

### Stronger specimen

> **Route:** U.S. federal contract obligations, [USASpending API](https://api.usaspending.gov/).
>
> **Distinctive affordance:** Compare contract announcements with subsequent official obligations by program, organization and time. Especially useful for distinguishing a public procurement *opportunity* from recorded spending commitments.
>
> **Trigger example:** A listed supplier announces a multiyear agency contract; the desk wants to estimate whether reported exposure is already reflected in public awards.
>
> **Pivot:** Company disclosure → contract identifier → award records → action/obligation history → company recognition statement.
>
> **Known trap:** A potential contract ceiling is not revenue, and obligations can be modified or recoded. Name matching requires identifier confirmation.
>
> **Reason to revisit:** The route clarified a material claim that ordinary headlines did not. Try it on another case and keep both useful and disappointing results; no fixed trial count is required.
>
> **Validation:** Official API documentation reviewed; no live endpoint execution claimed in this example.

**Why it's better:** the resource entry teaches a **new move**, states its epistemic limits, and tells the next researcher when it is worthwhile. Ordinary Git commits preserve edits. Significant discoveries warrant a compact method-update record; no need to stamp every row with the same date.

## 6. Forecast that is possible to score later

### Weak

> This will probably be a big trend in the coming months. Confidence: 82%.

### Stronger specimen

> **Question F-31:** Will official Agency Q publish a final, effective rule affecting product category C on or before 2027-03-31?
>
> **As-of:** 2026-10-31 14:00 ET.
>
> **Probability:** 35% (illustrative judgment; not a calibrated model output).
>
> **Resolution:** Yes if the official public record contains the specified final rule with effective date on/before cutoff; otherwise No, subject to a predetermined cancellation/unresolvable rule.
>
> **Reference class:** Counts of similar prior proposals that became effective within the equivalent window, using a documented cohort.
>
> **Why not 70%:** No final-review notice yet; prior cohort typically took longer.
>
> **Would change my mind:** Formal proposed publication timeline, procedural withdrawal, or authoritative agency update.
>
> **Research significance:** This forecast informs a market-research catalyst watch; it does not score whether a policy is desirable.

**Why it's better:** explicit resolution, dated evidence state, baseline, and a natural later check. You can score it honestly and still ask whether it was useful to the operator.

## 7. Daily desk progress: expectations, intentions, and reality

This is a status **view**, not a new task orchestration tool:

| Operator expectation | Manager intention | Reality / next trigger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before bell: concise portfolio-aware briefing | Preserve morning output slot; synthesize overnight material | Report artifact drafted; awaiting source check |
| Continuous material-change coverage | One worker monitors selected official feeds | Worker active; next review when new filing arrives |
| Maintain rare-earth specialty | Compare capacity milestones and substitute pathways | Research worker active; independent challenge queued when slot frees |
| Weekly sector assessment | Reserve review time and use deep artifacts | Not due yet; candidate topics listed in FRONTIER |

**Why it's better:** this table avoids pretending that a plan is what the workers are actually doing. It also avoids creating a second scheduler that tells the PE manager which agents to run.

## Questions that help judge the work

A working report or field note should be inspectable against six questions—not a weighted fake accuracy score:

| Question | Good evidence of quality |
| --- | --- |
| **Relevant?** | Why this matters *to this operator now*, not to the internet generally |
| **Original?** | At least one meaningful primary-source step or distinctly useful synthesis |
| **Grounded?** | Direct sources, as-of time, source/method scope and important missing evidence |
| **Challenged?** | Strongest plausible contrary interpretation and whether it was investigated |
| **Actionable as research?** | Clear next question, monitoring trigger, review request or honest no-action conclusion |
| **Accumulative?** | Updates earlier claims and preserves useful corrections rather than re-writing yesterday's report |

A report may be excellent as a morning briefing without being an original investigation. A deep technical dossier may be excellent while having no urgent operator action. Judge the work **against its promised service**, not a single universal rubric.
